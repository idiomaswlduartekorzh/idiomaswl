## Method

I loaded every executable module from `icfes-mock-01.ts` through `icfes-mock-23.ts` and read every stimulus, passage, stem, option set and current key. I assessed grammar, idiomatic naturalness, clarity, key uniqueness and defensibility, distractor plausibility, stimulus–question–option coherence and the generated explanations. Deterministic scripts verified inventory, key bounds, duplicate normalized options, exact duplicates and answer-position distributions. Those checks supported the review but were not treated as evidence of semantic correctness.

Uncertain cases are explicitly marked `needs-adjudication`; I did not guess or silently alter an answer. The machine-actionable record with full evidence and proposed remediation is in `english-specialist.json`.

## Decision basis

The content bank cannot receive an English-specialist PASS in its current state:

1. The guided adapter assigns the wrong task model to every Part 1, Part 2 and Part 7 item. This makes **460 explanations/rationales structurally false**: 115 P1 notices are explained as vocabulary matching, 115 P2 vocabulary items are explained as notices and 230 P7 reading questions are explained as lexical cloze.
2. The adapter automatically labels all 1,035 generated records `editorialStatus: 'reviewed'` with a fixed review date despite the absence of a hash-bound semantic review.
3. `mock-20:p5q3` has **no valid answer**: the stem asks what students must do, but every option is a complete declarative sentence and the keyed option answers why, not what.
4. The answer-position distribution creates a learnable cue: B/C contain 145/161 P5 keys (90.1%), 100/115 P6 keys (87.0%) and 213/230 P7 keys (92.6%).
5. Multiple scored passages and keyed options contain material grammar, naturalness, factual-coherence or key-defensibility defects.

Earlier repository audits that passed structural or automated checks do not resolve these findings. Key bounds, schema validity and test success cannot establish English-language quality or semantic correctness.
