## ROLE A — Liliana, warehouse assistant

> **Formal.** She's the supervisor; two years together. You say *doña Amparo*; she uses your first name. **She starts.** About 7 turns · 7 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Monday, August 17, 7:35 a.m. Auto parts store on carrera 15, shutter half up. Third Monday in a row. Doña Amparo shuts the office door, then puts two things on the desk.

**You want** · Out of here with **no written warning in your file** — and, if you can, **with the second set of keys**.

**You can't**
1. Say why Saturday mornings are off limits. If they find out, the job goes to somebody else. Shut the day, don't open the reason.
2. Invent an excuse, or blame anyone in the store.
3. Close it with a promise — you made that one twice already. It has to name **what changes, exactly**.

**Only you know**
- English class, **Saturdays 8:00 to 12:00**, paid, no refund, no other group. B1 certificate, needed for paperwork. If the store hears you want out, the promotion is over. **Never said out loud.**
- The neighbor in apartment 3 — yours to offer, and you never did. Asking her costs you your Wednesdays.

**If you walk away with nothing** · A warning in your file — the only paper she has left. The August bonus, gone. Off the warehouse job list. And the keys go to somebody else, quietly.

### Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | Monday, August 17, 7:35 a.m. · the store opens at 7:00 |
| Late Mondays | August 3 · August 10 · today |
| Matías | daycare in Girón · drop-off at 6:40 |
| Your bus | it used to arrive at ten to seven · now, a quarter past seven |
| The road work | carrera 15 · it started on August 1 · twenty-five minutes more |
| The 5:50 bus | you're there at 6:45 · then the boy: thirty minutes alone at the daycare door |
| The neighbor in 3 | same daycare, every morning · you never asked her · her price: both girls, Wednesdays at five |
| August bonus | 80,000 pesos · gone with a written warning in your file · automatic, nobody decides it |
| The warehouse job | it opened in April · still nobody · opener = the one who signs the delivery note |
| The commitment sheet | entry time · start date · review date · **and one line: WHAT CHANGES** |

### Words you need here

| word | what it is | here |
|---|---|---|
| to be late | to arrive after the hour you must be there | third Monday — say it before she does |
| a written warning | a paper about a mistake that goes in your file | the one thing you can't leave with |
| your file | the papers about you that the company keeps | a warning here takes the bonus and the job |
| commitment sheet | a paper where the worker writes what will change | it's blank, and one line of it is yours |
| to fill out | to write the words in the empty space | the WHAT CHANGES line — she can't do it for you |
| road work | men fixing the street, and cars go slow | why the bus takes twenty-five minutes more |
| drop-off | the moment you leave someone somewhere and go | 6:40, and it's the hour that can't move |
| delivery note | the paper that says what comes on the truck | whoever opens up signs it — that's the job you want |
| bonus | extra money for doing something well | 80,000, and one paper takes it |
| to sign | to write your name on a paper | don't sign before you know which paper it is |

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** `[asks]` — she opens the door, you're the one who needs something — **3**, **5**, **6** (Saturday mornings: no other way out of that question), **8**, **2**. Not 4, not 7.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| what's on the desk | `What are the keys for?` | ask about what you walked into, before you talk about you |
| the papers | `Which paper is that, and who reads it?` | three papers sound the same and aren't |
| what changed | `My bus used to arrive at ten to seven — now it arrives at a quarter past.` | the two times together is what people understand |
| what changed | `The road work started on August 1.` | put a date on the cause instead of complaining |
| what can't move | `I leave Matías at the daycare at twenty to seven.` | the hour nothing can move around |
| this morning | `I'm sorry about this morning.` | apologize once, with the fact in front of you, then stop |
| the day you can't give | `Saturday mornings are difficult for me.` | shut the day without opening the reason |
| a mechanism | `I can start at…` | an hour instead of a promise |
| the price | `That costs me my Wednesdays.` | say out loud what you pay — that's point 3 of the close |

### You did it if
Open question in your **first** turn, about what's on that desk · one apology, then the whole reason — daycare, road work, bus, with times · you said what you really want · Saturday mornings shut **without the reason** · the WHAT CHANGES line is checkable next Monday: an hour, a bus, a name — not "I'll do better" · you can name the paper and who reads it · you said what it costs you.

## ROLE B — doña Amparo, supervisor

> **Formal.** Supervisor, two years together. She says *doña Amparo*; you use her name. **You start.** About 7 turns · 7 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Before you speak** · Put the **second set of keys** and the **blank commitment sheet** on the desk. Then talk. Skipping it makes this a lecture.

**Where you are** · Monday, August 17, 7:35 a.m. Store still shut. The Monday truck lands at 7:10, counting takes two — three Mondays now, alone.

**You want** · The second set of keys, **today**, to the only one here who knows warehouse and suppliers. Two things first: **the WHAT CHANGES line filled out**, **two mornings of receiving training**.

**You can't**
1. Fire her, cut her pay, pay extra hours. Not yours to give.
2. Hand over keys with nothing in writing, after three late Mondays. The other four would see.
3. Fill out the WHAT CHANGES line yourself. No line, no paper.

**Only you know**
- You covered the first two Mondays — never in the **incident form**. Write one today and the question turns on you: where are the other two?
- Alba, from the counter, already asked: never late, three blocks away, five months in, no stock training. The door, not the truck.
- What you **could** offer and won't: training **two Thursdays after closing, 6:30 to 8:30 p.m.** — two unpaid nights.

**If you walk away with nothing** · Two more years as the only one who opens and receives stock. And your own warning makes you explain the other two.

### Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | Monday, August 17, 7:35 a.m. · the store opens at 7:00 |
| The truck | Mondays at 7:10 · two people to unload and count against the delivery note |
| Late Mondays | August 3 · August 10 · today |
| The commitment sheet | entry time · start date · review date · **and one line: WHAT CHANGES** |
| What can be written, and there is no fourth | **1)** a written warning **in her file** · **2)** a note **in the store folder** · **3)** the commitment sheet and nothing else |
| The training | two mornings: counting against the delivery note, missing items, returns |
| When the warehouse is quiet | Saturdays, 8:00 to 12:00 |
| Review date | Monday, September 14 |
| The second set of keys | opener = the one who signs the delivery note |
| The others | four people in the store · they see everything |

### Words you need here

| word | what it is | here |
|---|---|---|
| delivery note | the paper that lists what comes on the truck | you count the boxes against it, and it takes two |
| to unload | to take the boxes off the truck | 7:10 on Mondays, three Mondays alone |
| to receive stock | to check what arrives and take it in | only you can do it — that's the whole problem |
| missing items | things on the paper that never came | half of what the training is about |
| returns | goods the store sends back to the supplier | the other half |
| training | mornings when you teach somebody the job | two of them, and you give them yourself |
| incident form | the company paper where late days get written | the two Mondays you left out of it |
| written warning | a paper about a mistake, in the worker's file | you can write it, and it costs you too |
| commitment sheet | a paper where the worker writes what will change | the only paper you can sign safely |
| review date | the day you look again and see if it worked | September 14 — it turns a promise into a check |

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** `[grants]`, **3**, **7** `[grants]` for what isn't yours, **8**, **2**, and above all **6**: one question today you won't answer. Not 4, not 5.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| the thing on the desk | `Sit down. I want to give you these keys.` | say what you came for before you talk about this morning |
| the thing on the desk | `This line is for you, not for me.` | point at the blank line only she can fill out |
| clear the air | `I'm not angry.` | turn off the lecture she walked in expecting |
| the whole story | `What happened this morning?` | ask before you decide anything |
| not enough yet | `That helps, but I need…` | say it falls short without putting her down |
| your price | `Two mornings, and I teach you myself.` | the number and who does it, in one line |
| the rule, not the fight | `I have to write something.` | stand behind the obligation instead of arguing |
| why her | `You know this warehouse better than anybody.` | say why you're asking her and not somebody else |
| yes, with a condition | `If you…, I can give you the keys.` | tie what you give to what you need, in one sentence |

### You did it if
Keys and sheet on the desk before you spoke · you asked open twice and waited · you said why **her** · the WHAT CHANGES line came out **in her words**, checkable next Monday · a training date **you** can keep, and its price said out loud · nothing promised outside your hands, no lie about the two Mondays.

## The card — **its own screen** · role B only

> **Its own screen, behind a button. Never printed under the toolkit.**
> **Open it at the start of global turn 5** (B's third, because B starts: B, A, B, A, **B**). Role A never sees it — she only hears what she's told.

> **Alba knocks on the office door.** You step into the hallway for thirty seconds and come back. This one doesn't arrive on anybody's phone: it walks in.
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | What's being said outside | the four of them are talking about this morning |
> | The question one of them asked | why the other two Mondays are not in the incident form |

Whatever you sign now **has to be sayable in front of the other four**. A quiet arrangement is no use to you any more, and any schedule change they can see will have to be explained with something she gives back in the open. How much of this you tell her is your call.

**Si se lee antes de tiempo:** no se gana nada, se pierde. Quien la lee entra ya blindando su
secreto y convierte esto en un ultimátum de cinco turnos en el que la otra dice veintiuna
palabras. Se caen el jardín, la obra y el bus, que son justo lo que había que sacarle.

## Both screens — how it ends

**You finish when the two of you have said out loud these three things, and you agree on what they are:**

1. **What gets written, and where:** a written warning in her file · a note in the store folder · the commitment sheet and nothing else.
2. **What changes next Monday, exactly:** the entry time, **the mechanism** — who takes the boy, which bus, who opens up — and the review date.
3. **When and where the receiving training happens, and what it costs each of you**, said by the one who pays it.

**And to really finish:** the commitment sheet on the desk gets signed, and the WHAT CHANGES line is read out loud the way it was written. If nobody can hear what happens next Monday at seven, it isn't finished.

**Three, or it isn't closed.** Point 2 stops you closing with a promise; point 3 stops you closing without looking at the price.

## After — both screens, in Spanish

1. ¿En qué turno se supo a qué había venido cada una? ¿Se dijo con palabras o se dedujo de algo que se puso encima de la mesa?
2. Lean en voz alta el renglón «qué cambia» tal como quedó. ¿Es un mecanismo o es una promesa? ¿Cómo lo sabrían el lunes que viene?
3. Cada una diga qué le costó lo que aceptó. Si a alguna no le costó nada, ¿quién lo pagó?

## Presupuesto — prosa por ficha, sin tablas

Contado sobre las líneas que no son tabla ni encabezado, con el mismo método que dio 307 y 339
en el molde (`fase7-modelo-ficha-en.md`). Tope de §11 para A1-A2: **350**.

**Nota de variedad, y no la escondo.** El encargo sugería `rota` en el vocabulario. `rota` es
británico; en americano es `schedule`, y §11 fija americano para los ocho escenarios. Aquí ni
uno ni otro entró en el vocabulario: ninguno de los dos roles necesita la palabra para llegar al
cierre —lo que se negocia es una hora de entrada y un mecanismo, no un cuadrante—, y la prueba de
§11 dice que entonces sale. `to cover` tampoco: aquí nadie cubre el turno de nadie. Sí entraron
las otras tres del encargo: `written warning`, `to be late` y `commitment sheet`.
