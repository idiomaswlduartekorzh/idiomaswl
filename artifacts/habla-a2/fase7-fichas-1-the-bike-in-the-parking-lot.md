## ROLE A — You're selling the bike

> **Polite, two strangers.** A deal in a lobby — nobody raises their voice. English has one *you*: the distance lives in the forms. **They start.** About 6 turns · 6 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Saturday, 10:15 a.m., lobby of your building in Cabecera. Three weeks on Marketplace, four trips down with it.

**You want** · The bike out of here **today**, **one payment**. Under 390,000 only if something else in the deal moves.

**You can't**
1. Sell under **390,000** with the new gears on. A number, not a preference.
2. Leave the bike in the lobby after today. The doorman, twice already.
3. Take the gears off here. Shop work, and the shop closes at 1:00.

**Only you know**
- The new gear set **fits the road bike you want next**. Old one back on = **330,000**, and you lose nothing. Say it in turn one and you give it away.
- **Lock** and **lights** — not in the ad, and nobody has named them.
- Fourth buyer in three weeks. The other three said the same about the tire.

**If you walk away with nothing** · Four floors on your shoulder again, another Saturday blocked for a fifth buyer with the same speech, and the road bike stays where it is.

### Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | Saturday, September 5, 10:15 a.m. |
| Ad price | 420,000 pesos · up three weeks |
| What it cost you | 680,000 pesos, sixteen months ago · receipt at home |
| At the shop last month | new gear set, brake pads, cable — 95,000 pesos · receipt in your pocket |
| The old gear set | it works · harder on the two small gears |
| The bike today | rear tire worn out · seat torn on one corner · scratch on the frame |
| Not in the ad | lock with two keys, 45,000 · front and rear lights, 35,000 |
| La Bici Roja, two blocks | rear tire fitted 38,000 · gear change 15,000 · closes 1:00 p.m. · pick-up around 3:00 |
| Your building | no bikes in the lobby after today · 4th floor, no elevator |
| Money | one payment · Nequi same day: 300 771 20 46 · road bike you want 750,000, you have 360,000 |

### Words you need here

| word | what it is | here |
|---|---|---|
| gear set | the parts that change how hard you pedal | the new one is on the bike, and it's your card |
| brake pads | the parts that press the wheel to stop it | part of the 95,000 you put in |
| worn out | used so much it doesn't work well | the rear tire — they'll say it before you do |
| to fit | to be the right size for something | *the new gears fit my next bike* — why you'd take them off |
| receipt | the paper the shop gives you with the price | your proof, and it's in your pocket |
| to throw something in | to add it to the deal for free | the lock, the lights — instead of cutting the number |
| lock | the metal chain you close a bike with | 45,000 that costs you nothing to give |
| to drop the price | to make the price lower | only when something else moves |
| the frame | the metal tubes, the body of the bike | where the scratch is |
| in cash | in bills, not by phone or card | one payment, today |

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** `[grants]` (they come to you), **2**, **4** `[jargon]` — *gear set*, *brake pads*, *cable* are your words, and someone who came by bus may not have them — **6** (offer the old gears back and they'll ask why: that's where the secret leaks), **7** `[grants]` and **8**. Not 3: the hard words are yours. Not 5: yours isn't a reason, it's a receipt.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| how it's paid | `Cash, or part by Nequi?` | you set out the ways to be paid, instead of waiting |
| the price | `I paid … for it, and I have the receipt.` | back the number with money you can show |
| the price | `I can't go under … with these gears on.` | plant the number without closing the door |
| today's clock | `It can't stay here after today.` | say what pushes you, without asking for pity |
| today's clock | `The shop closes at one.` | put the clock that squeezes you both on the table |
| the parts | `I can put the old gear set back on.` | take out of the deal the piece that's worth something to you |
| the parts | `The lock and the lights are not in the ad.` | add something instead of cutting the number |
| moving the deal | `If you …, I can …` | the price moves only when something else moves |
| asking about theirs | `How are you going to take it home?` | open question — transport is half the deal and you know nothing about it |

### You did it if
The bike leaves the lobby today, and you both said **at what time and with whom** · **one payment**, said out loud: how much and how · they said out loud how the rear tire is · every time the price went down, something inside the deal changed · you asked an open question and got a fact you didn't have · nobody found out you can go lower by keeping the gears.

## ROLE B — You came for the bike

> **Polite, two strangers.** A deal in a lobby — nobody raises their voice. English has one *you*: the distance lives in the forms. **You start.** About 6 turns · 6 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Saturday, 10:15 a.m., lobby of a building in Cabecera. You wrote last night, came by bus. Half a minute on the rear tire, then hello.

**You want** · The bike — or a day and a time to pick it up — and **transport said out loud**. Cap: **350,000 cash, bike alone**.

**You can't**
1. Pay over **350,000 in cash for the bike alone**. A number, not a feeling.
2. Move the 70,000 in Nequi unless it buys something **inside the deal**.
3. Say this is the only one that works for you. Say it and nothing moves.

**Only you know**
- Three weeks looking, **four bikes seen**. Only one in your size, only one with a receipt.
- Brother-in-law through Cabecera **today, 6:00 p.m., with a truck** — confirmed last night. Without it, no way to get the bike out of here.
- Nequi money already counted: tire, seat, lock. Not for the price.

**If you walk away with nothing** · Three weeks of looking, to start over. The only one in your size with papers stays here, and the bus every day.

### Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | Saturday, September 5, 10:15 a.m. |
| On you | 350,000 pesos in cash |
| In Nequi | 70,000 pesos — for the bike: tire, seat, lock |
| Ad price | 420,000 pesos |
| What you see | rear tire worn out · seat torn on one corner · scratch on the frame |
| To make it usable | about 70,000: a new rear tire and a new seat |
| Getting it out of here | brother-in-law's truck · Cabecera, 6:00 p.m. · confirmed last night |
| If not the truck | car with an app, about 22,000 · or ride it home on that tire |
| Where you live | Floridablanca · new job in Cañaveral |
| The bus | 6,400 a day, round trip · 32,000 a week |

### Words you need here

| word | what it is | here |
|---|---|---|
| second-hand | not new — someone used it before you | what you came to buy, and why the price isn't the ad price |
| worn out | used so much it doesn't work well | the rear tire — your first argument, and you say it early |
| torn | with a cut or a hole in it | the seat, on one corner |
| a scratch | a thin line where the paint came off | small, but you count it |
| the frame | the metal tubes, the body of the bike | where the scratch is |
| to fix | to make something work again | the tire and the seat, before you can use it |
| to come with | to be included in the price | *does the lock come with it?* — how you add instead of asking for less |
| in cash | in bills, not by phone or card | the 350,000 in your pocket, and it's your number |
| round trip | going and coming back | the bus: 6,400 a day, both ways |
| to pick something up | to go somewhere later and take it | if the bike doesn't leave now, you need a day and a time |

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** `[asks]`, **2**, **3** `[receives]` — they'll throw *gear set*, *brake pads*, *cable* at you and you don't have to know those: ask — **5** (yours has a number: the bus), **6** (you have something you can't say) and **8** (before you accept a figure). Not 4, not 7 — the hard words are theirs, and you don't grant.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| how you pay | `I have … in cash, right now.` | put your number on the table as a fact, not an offer |
| how you pay | `The Nequi money is for the tire and the seat.` | say what the other money is for, before they ask for it |
| the bike | `The rear tire is worn out.` | name the fault out loud — that's what moves a price without haggling |
| the bike | `A new tire and a new seat — that's about …` | put the cost of fixing it on the table |
| the price | `That's more than I can pay today.` | turn the figure down without starting a haggle |
| moving the deal | `Maybe we can …` | offer another way when the price stops moving |
| moving the deal | `Does the lock come with it?` | ask for something more inside the deal instead of asking for less |
| asking about theirs | `Why are you selling it?` | open question — brings out what isn't in the ad |
| asking about theirs | `What did you change on it?` | open question — asks about the work done, not the price |

### You did it if
You leave with the bike, or with a pick-up day and time, and **who moves it** said out loud · you stayed under **350,000 cash** for the bike alone · Nequi money moved only for something inside the deal, and you said so · you said out loud how the rear tire is · you asked an open question and got a fact you didn't have · nobody found out this is the only one that works for you.

## The card — separate screen · ROLE B only

> **Its own screen, behind a button. Never printed under the toolkit.**
> **Open it at the end of global turn 5** (B's third, because B starts).
> Role A never sees it: A only knows what B tells them.

> **WhatsApp · 10:34 a.m.**
> *No truck today. I'm going to Barrancabermeja. Sorry.*
> — my brother-in-law

**Si se lee antes de tiempo** (nota de diseño, no va en pantalla): no se gana nada, se pierde.
Quien la lee sabe desde el primer turno que no tiene transporte, y tiene que negociarlo **antes**
de haber asegurado el precio, enseñando su punto débil cuando el otro conserva toda la palanca.
Las alternativas cuestan lo mismo leídas antes o después.

## Both screens — how it ends

**You finish when the two of you say the whole deal out loud, with these four facts, and check that you're both saying the same thing:**

1. **The final price and how it's paid:** all cash, or how much cash and how much by Nequi.
2. **What the bike leaves with:** new gears or old gears, and what else is inside the deal.
3. **Who moves it, at what time, and how far.**
4. **The rear tire:** who fixes it, where, and who pays.

**One of the four missing and you're not done.** Say all four and match, and it's closed.

## After — both screens, in Spanish

1. ¿En qué turno dejó de moverse el precio y empezó a moverse **lo que entraba en el trato**?
   Búsquenlo: es el turno donde la conversación cambió de tema sin cambiar de tema.
2. Cada uno tenía un dato que le convenía no soltar de entrada. ¿Cuál era el del otro? ¿En qué
   momento apareció, y qué pregunta lo hizo aparecer?
3. Vuelvan a decir los cuatro datos del cierre, cada uno con sus palabras, sin mirar la pantalla.
   Si alguno de los cuatro sale distinto, ahí estaba el malentendido.
