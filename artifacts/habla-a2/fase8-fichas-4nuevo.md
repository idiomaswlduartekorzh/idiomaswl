# Escenario 4 (nuevo) · `the-pot-is-already-on` — las dos fichas en inglés

Las dos fichas del escenario que sustituye a `a-charge-i-did-not-make`. **El motor no se toca**:
conflicto, asimetría, carta, salidas y cierre son los de `fase8-escenario-4nuevo.md`. Aquí solo se
escribe la ficha, contra el molde `fase7-modelo-ficha-en.md` y §11 del blueprint.

Caja común: `artifacts/habla-a2/caja-de-herramientas-a2.md`. **Inglés americano.** Cada ficha va en
su propia URL y se lee sola. La carta vive en pantalla aparte, y aquí es del **rol A**.

`recomendar` + `insistir` + `conceder-con-condición` · poder **a>b** · arranca **A** · desenlace
**acuerdo-parcial** · **7 min** · 9 turnos por rol (18 globales) · **A = Fabián**, **B = Duván**.

**Y una cosa que esta ficha hace y las dieciséis anteriores no.** Las dieciséis usan la misma
plantilla rellenada: siete fórmulas de encabezado idénticas en 16 de 16, y once no añaden ni un
bloque propio. Aquí los encabezados salen del patio —el fuego, la moto, las horas de la olla— y
cada rol trae **un bloque que no existe en el resto del set**: el reloj de la olla en A, el de la
moto en B. La pieza obligatoria está entera; lo que cambia es de quién es la voz que la nombra.

## ROLE A — Fabián, and the pot is yours

> **No boss here.** You cook. He came at nine to help. **You speak first.** 9 turns · 7 minutes.
> **One screen, one player.** Keep it turned away. Don't read from it.

**The patio, 11:20 a.m.** · Sunday, September 20, in Girón. You lit the fire at nine. On Friday everybody agreed to eat here at one and go to the river after.

**What you're after** · One number before the fire gets to twelve: how many eat here at one. The river comes after lunch, not instead of it.

**Three things you won't do**
1. The pot stays on the fire. Raw cassava and boiling water are the reason, not stubbornness.
2. You don't say the group did wrong. You need them back at one, and he asks them.
3. Nothing leaves the fridge before you have a number and a name. Put **two ways** on the table **before** the yes.

**Nobody out there knows this**
- You know the pot isn't lunch yet. Cassava and plantain go in at twelve, and at 11:40 there's only broth.
- You took the second chicken out at seven and cut it. In before twelve, it feeds twelve. Not in, it goes bad tonight.
- You can't put it back: Saturday's rice and chicken are in there, and that container is your Monday and Tuesday lunch.
- You got up at six, you haven't eaten, and you haven't said so.

**If he rides off with nothing settled** · Twelve plates and nobody at the table. The chicken goes bad tonight. And the same people come back to this patio next Sunday.

### What the fire is doing while you talk

You look at it every twenty minutes. That's firewood, not habit.

## ROLE B — Duván, and the bike leaves at 11:40

> **No boss here — but the house, the fire and the food are his.** You came at nine and stayed. **He speaks first.** 9 turns · 7 minutes.
> **One screen, one player.** Keep it turned away. Don't read from it.

**The patio, and one foot outside it** · Sunday, September 20, 11:20 a.m., in Girón. The heat took six of your friends down to the river at ten. You stayed.

**What you need before you go** · Édgar passes at 11:40, and that bike is the only way to the river before the afternoon. You want to be on it, with food in your hands.

**Three lines you don't cross**
1. You don't show up empty-handed. You wrote in the chat that lunch was coming.
2. You don't correct the ten o'clock message until something is in your hands. You give that truth once.
3. Only what fits between your feet goes on that bike.

**What you haven't said yet**
- You know Marcela's car isn't coming back: at 11:11 she wrote that she stays there until four. You told him at ten that everybody would be here at one, and you haven't shown him.
- You're leaving at 11:40 on Édgar's bike. He thinks you're staying until one.
- You know the six ate at seven and that nothing is open there on a Sunday. That card is your strongest and it cuts both ways: play it early, and he answers that they should walk back.

**If you leave at 11:40 with nothing** · Six people at a river with no food, no store, no car until four — and your message sent them there. Behind you, lunch for twelve and one man alone at the fire.

## Both screens — how it ends

**Six things get said out loud, three by each of you. Nobody says the other person's three.**

**Only A can say:** what goes in the pot at twelve, and for how many · what leaves this patio now, and what stays · what A needs at one o'clock and can't do with the fire on.

**Only B can say:** how many really come back, and before what time · how they get here without Marcela's car · what B tells the six at the river, and before what hour.

**Together, once:** the time of the second round, and who's at the gate when the first people arrive.

**And one thing stays open on purpose:** who doesn't eat with the others, and who fixes it. Say it with a name and a time.

**Three rules, and they're what make this one different from the other seven:**
- Nobody repeats the other's line. Say his line for him and it doesn't count — he says it again.
- Nobody nods. *Sure*, *okay* and *fine* are none of the six lines.
- Nobody writes anything down and nobody reads anything out. This is a patio. There's no number to confirm and nothing to sign.

## Lo que se hizo distinto, y por qué

**1 · La voz.** Ninguna de las siete fórmulas de encabezado del set sobrevive tal cual. Ni
«Where you are», ni «You want», ni «You can't», ni «Only you know», ni «If you walk away with
nothing», ni la cita de registro, ni la línea de pantalla. En su lugar, encabezados que solo
tienen sentido en este patio: *The patio, 11:20 a.m.* · *What you're after* · *Three things you
won't do* · *Nobody out there knows this* · *If he rides off with nothing settled* en A; y en B,
que está de salida, *The patio, and one foot outside it* · *What you need before you go* ·
*Three lines you don't cross* · *What you haven't said yet* · *If you leave at 11:40 with
nothing*. Las cinco secciones fijas también cambian de nombre por rol: *the numbers you're
cooking with* contra *the numbers you're carrying*, *Words for the fire* contra *Words for the
road*, *Out loud, in this patio* contra *Out loud, before the bike*.

**2 · Un bloque propio por rol, que el resto del set no tiene.** Once de las dieciséis fichas
anteriores no añaden ni uno. Aquí cada rol trae su reloj físico, y son relojes distintos:
**«What the fire is doing while you talk»** en A —de las 9:00 a la tarde, con el minuto en que la
segunda gallina deja de servir— y **«The bike, and what fits»** en B —de las 11:40 a las 4:00, con
lo que cabe entre los pies—. Son los dos relojes que hacen que `insistir` sea cortés y no grosero
(§0 del diseño), y por eso van en la ficha y no en una nota del profesor.

**3 · Prosa legible, no telegrama.** La corrección de hoy se aplicó línea a línea: nada de
«if nobody goes: her news, tonight». Y la prueba de calcabilidad se pasó **después**, que es el
orden que enseñó la ronda anterior: las cuatro frases del dato oculto de A y las tres de B se
reescribieron en segunda persona —*You took the second chicken out at seven*, *You know Marcela's
car isn't coming back*— justamente porque en tercera persona se podían decir tal cual.

**4 · El vocabulario, veinte entradas y ninguna de trámite.** Las diez de A son de cocina y fuego,
las diez de B de transporte, ocio y cuerpo: los cuatro campos con **cero** entradas en el set
actual. Ninguna glosa pasa de una línea, todas son más simples que la palabra que definen, y la
columna `here` es nota de propósito en las veinte —*the clock on the second chicken*, *one word
for your first no*—: ni una comilla, ni un ejemplo, ni un pronombre con verbo conjugado al
principio.

**5 · Inglés americano y A2 verificado.** *Firewood*, *cooler*, *bike*; ni *cool box* ni *motorbike* ni *petrol*. Cero
present perfect de duración con *for/since* en prosa —de ahí *You took the second chicken out at
seven* en vez de *has been out since seven*, que sí vive en la tabla, donde es una nota—, cero
pasiva, cero pregunta incrustada, cero `could` de cortesía.
