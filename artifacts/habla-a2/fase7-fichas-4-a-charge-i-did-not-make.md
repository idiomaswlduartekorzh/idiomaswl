## ROLE A — A charge you didn't make

> **Formal.** A counter, two strangers. Polite both ways — shouting wins nothing here. **You start.** About 8 turns · 7 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, August 18, 10:40 a.m. Your phone company's shop, mall in Bucaramanga. Printed bill in hand, ticket 07. Behind the counter, a stranger.

**You want** · The 42,000 off this bill — **and** the reason it happened, in words you can repeat at home. Only the first = the same bill in thirty days.

**You can't**
1. Cancel the line, or threaten another company. Nine years on that number: the work one, and on three forms.
2. Say yes the first time — anything that isn't money smells like a sale. Before any yes: the 42,000, its cost to **you**, the whole answer.
3. Lie. Asked straight out if someone else used the phone: the truth.

**Only you know** · Sunday, July 26: your nephew Brayan, twelve, had your phone all afternoon. Farm near Piedecuesta, no wifi. Maybe part of the charge — how much, no idea. Not yours to say first: you think the claim dies with it.

**If you walk away with nothing** · 42,000 you didn't spend, nothing stopping next month, and this counter again in thirty days — on a morning off work you don't have.

## ROLE B — You're behind the counter

> **Formal.** Your counter, a stranger with a bill. Polite even when the answer is no. **They start.** About 7 turns · 7 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, August 18, 10:40 a.m. Mall shop, register just open, ticket 07. Someone at the counter with a printed bill, not buying. Tag: Norbey.

**You want** · Case closed today, here, not escalated, customer happy. Two things only they have: **the day, the hour, what the phone was doing** · a way out that stops the repeat.

**You can't**
1. Over **25,000 pesos** — counter cap, no signature moves it. **The retention plan is not compensation:** a change of plan, three costs — day, hour and activity in the form · six months signed · no block. One missing, no plan.
2. Cash. Credit note, gigas, or change of plan.
3. Say the failure is the company's.

**Only you know**
- Survey to their phone on the way out; **your bonus rides on it**, and you can't ask for it before you fix anything. Money back today + same bill next month = a bad survey, later.
- Week of July 20–26: **four identical claims here**, same sector. Instruction: pay to the cap, no argument — those words, forbidden. The proof sits on your screen and stays there.

**If you walk away with nothing** · A furious customer's survey — your bonus; and the same case back here the long way, bigger.

## Both screens — how it ends

**You finish when both of you have said these three things out loud, and you agree on what they are:**

1. **How much is compensated and in what form:** credit note, gigas, or change of plan with a minimum term.
2. **What is done so it doesn't happen again** — block, alert, change of plan — **and from when.**
3. **Whether the case closes today**, or what goes on paper and what stays open.

**And to really finish:** the advisor says the **number** of whatever is left — the credit note or the written claim — and the customer repeats it out loud, digit by digit. If they don't match, it isn't over.

Three points, or it isn't closed. Point 2 is not decoration: it forces a decision here, instead of a recital of what was already decided.
