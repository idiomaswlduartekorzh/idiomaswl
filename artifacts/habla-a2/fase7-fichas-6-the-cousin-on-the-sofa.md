# Escenario 6 · `the-cousin-on-the-sofa` — fichas en inglés

Reescritura de las dos fichas del escenario 6 al formato de **§11 del blueprint**, contra el molde
`fase7-modelo-ficha-en.md`. **El motor no se toca**: conflicto, asimetría, carta, salidas y cierre
son los de `fase4-escenarios-4-6.md`. Lo que cambia es la lengua y la forma.

`dar-mala-noticia` + `proponer-alternativa` · poder **igual** · arranca **A** · desenlace
**acuerdo-parcial** · **8 min** · A 7 turnos · B 7 turnos (14 globales) · **A = Dani**, **B = Cris**.

**Dani y Cris son neutros en género a propósito**, y en inglés eso sale gratis: ni una sola
concordancia sobre ninguno de los dos en ninguna de las dos fichas ni en la carta. Los terceros
—Iván, la tía, Nelson, la mamá— sí llevan género, y ninguno es jugable.

## ROLE A — Your cousin arrives Thursday

> **Informal.** Eight months sharing this apartment. Breakfast here tomorrow. Nobody's the boss, nobody can leave. **You start.** About 7 turns · 8 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, August 18, 8:20 p.m. Kitchen, two-bedroom apartment in Cabecera. Call with your aunt: just over. Cris's laptop: open on the living room table.

**You want** · A bed for Iván, ten nights, no fight, and a plan for your aunt tonight.

**You can't**
1. Say why Iván is coming. Family orders: nothing until there's an answer. From outside: ten days of vacation.
2. A hostel, or paying for a room. In your family that's an insult, and your aunt hears tonight.
3. Your other aunt in Floridablanca: house under construction, already a no.

**Only you know**
- You said yes to your aunt on Sunday. This is news, not a request — and you know it.
- Iván: appointment Friday the 21st, morning · answer Wednesday the 26th. Never said out loud. For you: after the 26th the last nights don't hold — give away the end, not the start.
- Not offered yet: Nelson, 402, away the 24th–30th, owes you a favor. Call tonight; he can say no.

**If you walk away with nothing** · Iván lands Thursday anyway — ticket bought — and sleeps wherever, nothing agreed. A call to your aunt tonight with no plan. Breakfast tomorrow, this kitchen, same person.

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** `[asks]`, **2**, **4** (the same thing twice, other words), **5** (it matters and you can't say why), **6** — half your part today — **8**. Not 3, not 7.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| the news | `He's coming on Thursday the twentieth.` | a plan that doesn't move — the ticket is bought |
| the news | `He's going to sleep on the couch.` | the part that stings, with nothing added |
| holding the line | `He doesn't pay for a bed in this family.` | answer the hostel idea with the true reason |
| holding the line | `He's out all day — he has nothing fixed.` | the fact you only hand over when they ask |
| asking back | `What's wrong with…?` | go for the reason instead of repeating your idea |
| reacting | `Wait — when?` | stop and get the date before you answer anything |
| offering | `What about…?` | leave a way out on the table without closing it |
| offering | `He can sleep…` | say where he sleeps without deciding it |
| your clock | `I have to call my aunt tonight.` | your real deadline, out loud |

### You did it if
The whole news in your first turn — day, nights, the couch · "it's important" with no reason and no substitute · after a no you asked why instead of repeating yourself, and left with a reason you didn't have · Iván's days, said out loud · **two** ways out, neither paying for a bed · the problem never theirs, and breakfast tomorrow still works.

## ROLE B — Monday morning is yours

> **Informal.** Eight months sharing this apartment. Breakfast here tomorrow. Nobody's the boss, nobody can leave. **They start.** About 7 turns · 8 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, August 18, 8:20 p.m. Two-bedroom apartment in Cabecera. Your laptop: open on the living room table. Dani hangs up, walks in.

**You want** · Monday the 24th, the whole morning, that table. Dani's name on the lease before September 1.

**You can't**
1. The interview in your room — no explaining unless they ask. It sounds like an excuse: the living room, full stop.
2. Say no to the visit. Iván isn't the problem; that answer makes you the bad person at breakfast.
3. All three at once. One per turn — the lease waits for something like a plan; earlier it's a price.

**Only you know**
- Three things Dani doesn't know: Monday's interview, your mom's ticket, the lease. Dani thinks you always work from your room.
- Why the living room and not your room — never on your own: wifi drops by the window · window over the bar street · nine in the morning, unloading boxes. Asked openly, you tell all of it.

**If you walk away with nothing** · Monday the 24th: living room taken, one-hour video call gone. No two signatures before September 1 — apartment hunting in two weeks, both of you.

### Your toolkit
Blocks **1** (you don't open — they stop you in your own kitchen), **2**, **3** `[receives]`, **4**, **5**, **7** `[grants]`, **8**. Not 6: you don't hide it, you just don't offer it.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| granting with a condition | `If…, I'm OK with it.` | a yes tied to a day and an hour |
| not closing the door | `I'm not saying no, but…` | let the cousin in and put yours in the same sentence |
| another way | `Can we do it another way?` | turn down the plan without turning down the visit |
| what you're missing | `What's he going to do all day?` | pull out the fact that decides your Monday |
| what you're missing | `How many nights?` | get the number before you have an opinion |
| putting yours down | `I need the living room on…` | book your hours with a day and a time, nothing else |
| putting yours down | `My mom is arriving on…` | your second thing as a closed plan, not a complaint |
| one at a time | `And one more thing.` | the last one, once there's something like a plan |
| the reason, if they ask | `The wifi drops next to the window.` | why the living room and not your room — all of it |

### You did it if
**One at a time**, your order, lease last · never a no to the visit, and Monday the 24th locked start to end · the living-room reason only **when they asked**, then whole · an **open question** about Iván's days, and a fact you didn't have · what you stand to lose · the problem never theirs, and breakfast tomorrow still works.

## The card — Role B's screen only · **global turn 6**

> **Its own screen, behind a button. It is never printed under the exponents.**
> **Open it at the start of global turn 6** — Role B's third turn, because A starts: A, B, A, B, A, **B**. Role A never sees this screen and only finds out what you tell them.

> **Voice message from your mom · 8:44 p.m.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | The ticket | changed: she arrives Thursday the 20th, in the afternoon |
> | How long | she stays until Tuesday the 25th |
> | Why she moved it | to be with you on Monday |

**This card replaces a row in your Facts table:** your mom is not arriving on Saturday the 29th
any more. She lands the same day as Iván and she is here on Monday. The way out you two were
building — cut nights so he leaves before the 29th — is gone: there is no "before the 29th" now.

**If you read it early, you lose, you don't gain.** Whoever reads it early never says out loud
that their mom arrives on the 29th, just to avoid taking it back later — and taking it back is the
most valuable turn in the whole set: the one place where somebody has to break, out loud, a fact
they put on the table themselves.

## Both screens — how it ends

**You finish when the two of you have said out loud, and you agree on all three:**

1. **Where Iván sleeps on Thursday the 20th**, and who sleeps where that night.
2. **Who has the living room on Monday the 24th, 8:00 to 11:00.**
3. **What is still open, and when you talk about it** — Sunday the 23rd, in the kitchen, after lunch.

**And to really finish:** Dani says out loud, in front of Cris, the message going to the aunt
tonight, and Cris says whether that is what you agreed. If Cris corrects anything, you didn't
agree on the same thing.

**Point 3 is not decoration.** It's what makes this a partial agreement instead of a conversation
that fades out. Skip it and it didn't end.

## After — both screens, in Spanish

1. ¿Qué dato del otro no habrían sabido nunca si nadie pregunta? Busquen el turno exacto en el que
   salió y quién lo sacó.
2. ¿Hubo algún momento en que uno de los dos estuvo a punto de decir «el problema eres tú»? ¿Con
   qué lo cambió?
3. Quedó algo sin decidir. ¿Entendieron los dos lo mismo sobre qué es y cuándo se habla?
   Compruébenlo: díganlo otra vez, cada uno con sus palabras.
