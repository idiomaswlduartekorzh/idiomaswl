## ROLE A — You have no appointment to give

> **Formal.** A clinic front desk: distance and calm, also for a no. The distance lives in `I'm afraid…`, `Let me…`, not in the pronoun. **They start.** 6 turns · 6 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, 4:20 p.m., front desk of a dental clinic in Cabecera. A walk-in, hand on the jaw.

**You want** · That person out with three things: a date in the book, a verified cell number, something for tonight.

**You can't**
1. Ask Dr. Restrepo anything before 5:00 — she's mid-consultation. She writes to you; you don't call her.
2. Say a word about another patient's appointment. Not whose, not why.
3. Send anyone away without a written date **and** something for tonight. The administrator checks the book at closing.

**Only you know**
- The 5:20 slot today: a patient who has missed twice. You can say something may open up. Never whose, never why.
- Dr. Restrepo leaves at 6:30, stayed late Monday. You don't ask her for favors — but for a broken tooth she doesn't say no. The key here.
- Three no-shows this week. Every empty chair counts against reception — a patient sent elsewhere too.

**If you walk away with nothing** · A fourth empty chair, a plan patient lost to the office on the corner. Both on your name.

### Your toolkit
All eight blocks — nobody else in the set does this for a living. **1** `[grants]`, **2**, **3**, **4** `[jargon]` — *referral note*, *emergency check*, *X-ray* are yours, not theirs — **5**, **6** — an appointment you can't name: 6 is your answer when they push — **7** `[grants]`, **8**.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| the book | `The first free time is Thursday at seven.` | bad news with the fact in front, not with a no |
| the book | `Something may open up later, but I can't promise anything.` | a door left open without naming anyone |
| their side | `What happened?` | **open question** — without it, the fact that turns the scenario never comes out |
| the other way out | `You should go there before eight.` | one road, with its hour on it |
| the plan | `The plan pays for it here, and only here.` | what they lose by leaving, without telling them to stay |
| the number | `Can you say the number one by one?` | make them dictate it digit by digit |
| the number | `Let me repeat it: …` | read back what you wrote, so they confirm it |
| tonight | `Put something cold on it for ten minutes.` | a concrete instruction instead of general advice |
| tonight | `If the pain goes to your ear, go to the emergency room tonight.` | the fourth point of the close |

### You did it if
**Day, hour and branch** out loud — plus the **treatment date here** if you sent them elsewhere · the cell number **digit by digit**, read back and confirmed · one thing for tonight, said back in their words · **one open question before you proposed anything**, and it changed the conversation · **when not to wait**, out loud · nothing said about another patient.

## ROLE B — You need to be seen today

> **Formal.** Somebody else's counter, nobody you know. Distance even when it hurts: `Can you help me, please?`. **You start.** 6 turns · 6 minutes.
> **Your screen only.** Don't show it. Don't read from it.

**Where you are** · Tuesday, 4:20 p.m., a dental clinic in Cabecera. Off work in Girón at 3:30, no appointment, hand on your jaw.

**You want** · To be seen **today**. If not: a date you can really make, plus something for tonight. On the table, only what's yours — time, a day, your pocket.

**You can't**
1. Take a weekday appointment before **8:30 a.m.** Girón warehouse at 7:00; **Thursdays the truck at 6:30** — storeroom key yours, nobody else's.
2. Come back another day for the same line. This is your free afternoon.

**Only you know**
- Sunday: a piece of your lower back tooth broke, and the edge cuts your tongue. Nobody told you to hide it — the pain is what matters, the piece looks like a detail. Out it comes if they ask something open.
- Last time you gave your sister's number as the contact. Hence no reminder, ever. You don't remember.

**If you walk away with nothing** · The pain, no date, nothing for tonight. And anywhere else the treatment is out of pocket — the plan works only here.

### Your toolkit
Five blocks, the shortest in the set on purpose: not your ground, not your words, and it hurts. **1** `[asks]`, **2**, **3** `[receives]` — *emergency check*, *referral note*, *first consultation* coming at you: ask — **5** — hurting isn't enough, say what **you** lose — **8**. Not 4, not 6, not 7: their words, nothing hidden, nothing to grant.

### Say it here — use it or don't

| function | form | what it does here |
|---|---|---|
| what it's like | `It's worse than yesterday.` | describe by comparing, which is how pain gets described |
| what it's like | `It hurts more with something cold.` | the detail that gets you taken seriously |
| what you can't do | `I can't come at seven, because I open the warehouse.` | close an hour with the reason attached, not with a wish |
| what you can offer | `I can wait here.` | your time on the table — the one thing you have today |

### You did it if
A date you can **really** make, said with your schedule in hand · **two things of yours** on the table — waiting, a day that works, money — before anyone asked · your number, digit by digit, read back · what to do tonight, and **when not to wait** · **what the pain is like**, not only that it hurts · what you pay, or that you don't.

## The card — **its own screen** · role A only

> **Pantalla propia, detrás de un botón. No se imprime debajo del andamiaje.**
> **Se abre al terminar el turno global 5** (el tercero del rol B, porque arranca B).
> El rol B no la ve nunca: solo se entera de lo que el rol A le cuente.

> **Dr. Restrepo · 4:52 p.m.**
>
> | Today | one more patient, 6:15 p.m., between two appointments |
> |---|---|
> | First I need two things | the exact problem + the date for the full treatment |
> | Deadline | confirm before 5:30 p.m. |

**Si se lee antes de tiempo:** no se gana nada. La tarea es la misma leída antes o después —hay
que conseguir el motivo y la fecha, y eso solo se consigue hablando— y adelantarla sale mal: una
pregunta por la urgencia en el primer turno se contesta con «me duele mucho», y la pregunta se
gasta. El dato pivote aparece cuando hay conversación, no cuando hay prisa.

## Both screens — how it ends

**You finish when the two of you say the whole plan out loud and check that it matches, with these four things:**

1. **Day, hour and branch** of the appointment, and what time you have to be there.
2. **How much gets paid, and when** (or "nothing, the plan covers it").
3. **The cell number**, said out loud and confirmed one digit at a time.
4. **What happens with the pain until then, and when not to wait.**

**One of the four missing and it isn't finished.** If both of you say the four and they match, it closed.

## After — both screens, in Spanish

1. Hubo un momento en que la conversación cambió de prioridad. ¿Cuál fue **la pregunta** que lo provocó? ¿Era abierta o era de sí o no?
2. El jueves a las 7:00 no era un capricho: era un imposible con dos números detrás. ¿Los dos entendieron cuáles eran esos dos números? Díganlos ahora.
3. El celular es el único dato del cierre que se puede decir mal sin que nadie se entere. Repítanlo los dos, dígito a dígito, y comprueben si coincide con lo que quedó anotado.
