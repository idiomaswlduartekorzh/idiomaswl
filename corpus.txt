# ESCUCHA — Sam’s Corner

## ep01 · The Empty Shop
  You rented it. You actually rented the shop, and you didn’t tell me until this morning.
    ~ Lo alquilaste. Alquilaste el local de verdad, y no me lo dijiste hasta esta mañana.
  I signed the papers yesterday. The owner wanted a fast answer, so I decided on the same day.
    ~ Firmé los papeles ayer. El dueño quería una respuesta rápida, así que decidí el mismo día.
  How much is the rent?
    ~ ¿Cuánto es el alquiler?
  It’s low. Nobody rented it for two years, so we talked and he lowered it.
    ~ Es bajo. Nadie lo alquiló en dos años, así que hablamos y lo bajó.
  And the tiles?
    ~ ¿Y las baldosas?
  I washed one square yesterday. The colour is still there, under the paint.
    ~ Lavé un cuadro ayer. El color sigue ahí, debajo de la pintura.
  I walked past this door for twenty years. I never looked inside. I always crossed the street before the corner.
    ~ Pasé por esta puerta durante veinte años. Nunca miré adentro. Siempre cruzaba la calle antes de la esquina.
  Now you can look every day.
    ~ Ahora puedes mirar todos los días.

## ep02 · My Grandfather’s Key
  I brought you something. I kept it in a box for twenty years, with the papers of the bakery.
    ~ Te traje algo. Lo guardé en una caja veinte años, con los papeles de la panadería.
  A key? Is this the bakery key?
    ~ ¿Una llave? ¿Es la llave de la panadería?
  I got it in 1971. I opened that door every morning at five and I closed it at seven in the evening.
    ~ La conseguí en 1971. Abrí esa puerta cada mañana a las cinco y la cerré a las siete de la tarde.
  Did it work on this lock?
    ~ ¿Servía para esta cerradura?
  The lock is new. The key is only a story now.
    ~ La cerradura es nueva. La llave ahora es solo una historia.
  Then we can put the story on the wall.
    ~ Entonces podemos poner la historia en la pared.
  A wall of stories. People bring an object and we write the year on a small card next to it.
    ~ Un muro de historias. La gente trae un objeto y escribimos el año en una tarjeta al lado.
  I gave you the first one.
    ~ Yo te di el primero.

## ep03 · The First Problem
  The water didn’t stop. It ran all night under the floor, and I heard it from the street at midnight.
    ~ El agua no paró. Corrió toda la noche bajo el suelo, y la oí desde la calle a medianoche.
  Did you call the plumber last night?
    ~ ¿Llamaste al fontanero anoche?
  I called him at six. He didn’t answer until eight, and then he arrived at ten.
    ~ Lo llamé a las seis. No contestó hasta las ocho, y luego llegó a las diez.
  Did he fix the pipes?
    ~ ¿Arregló las tuberías?
  He fixed two pipes, but he didn’t finish the third one, because he didn’t have the right part.
    ~ Arregló dos tuberías, pero no terminó la tercera, porque no tenía la pieza.
  How much did it cost?
    ~ ¿Cuánto costó?
  Less than the water bill. The bad news is that he comes back on Thursday morning.
    ~ Menos que la factura del agua. La mala noticia es que vuelve el jueves por la mañana.

## ep04 · Rainy Saturday
  What were you doing when I called?
    ~ ¿Qué estabais haciendo cuando llamé?
  We were painting the back wall. Leo was standing on a chair with the roller in one hand.
    ~ Estábamos pintando la pared del fondo. Leo estaba de pie en una silla con el rodillo en una mano.
  And then it started to rain?
    ~ ¿Y entonces empezó a llover?
  It was raining hard while we were carrying the chairs inside.
    ~ Llovía fuerte mientras metíamos las sillas.
  I was holding two towels and a bucket at the same time, and I was not laughing.
    ~ Yo sostenía dos toallas y un cubo a la vez, y no me estaba riendo.
  The paint was still wet, so we were all very careful with the boxes near the door.
    ~ La pintura seguía mojada, así que íbamos todos con mucho cuidado con las cajas de la puerta.
  Was Max helping?
    ~ ¿Max ayudaba?
  Max was sleeping under the table. He wasn’t helping at all. He didn’t move once in two hours.
    ~ Max dormía debajo de la mesa. No ayudaba nada. No se movió ni una vez en dos horas.

## ep05 · A Better Name
  “The Corner” is shorter than “Sam’s Corner Bakery”, and people already call this corner that.
    ~ «The Corner» es más corto que «Sam’s Corner Bakery», y la gente ya llama así a esta esquina.
  Shorter, yes, but it’s less personal than my grandfather’s name.
    ~ Más corto, sí, pero es menos personal que el nombre de mi abuelo.
  “The Old Tiles” is more interesting than both.
    ~ «The Old Tiles» es más interesante que los dos.
  It’s also stranger. People will think we sell tiles.
    ~ También es más raro. La gente pensará que vendemos baldosas.
  The bakery was older than this street. The name should say that. My grandfather never wrote his name on the window.
    ~ La panadería era más antigua que esta calle. El nombre debería decirlo. Mi abuelo nunca escribió su nombre en la ventana.
  Then “Sam’s Corner”. It’s warmer and easier to remember.
    ~ Entonces «Sam’s Corner». Es más cálido y más fácil de recordar.
  That’s better than my idea.
    ~ Eso es mejor que mi idea.
  It’s better than all our ideas. We paint it tomorrow morning, before the new sign arrives.
    ~ Es mejor que todas nuestras ideas. Lo pintamos mañana por la mañana, antes de que llegue el cartel.

## ep06 · The Best Table
  This is the oldest table in the family. Your grandmother chose it in a market in 1968.
    ~ Esta es la mesa más antigua de la familia. La eligió tu abuela en un mercado en 1968.
  It’s the heaviest thing in the room too.
    ~ También es lo más pesado de la sala.
  Where is the brightest corner?
    ~ ¿Cuál es el rincón más luminoso?
  Near the window. That’s the best light in the whole shop, and the warmest corner in winter.
    ~ Junto a la ventana. Es la mejor luz de todo el local, y el rincón más cálido en invierno.
  Then the table goes there. It was always near a window.
    ~ Entonces la mesa va ahí. Siempre estuvo junto a una ventana.
  This is the most beautiful part of the room now. The wood looks different in the morning light.
    ~ Ahora esta es la parte más bonita de la sala. La madera se ve distinta con la luz de la mañana.
  And the most difficult to clean.
    ~ Y la más difícil de limpiar.
  The best things usually are.
    ~ Las mejores cosas suelen serlo.

## ep07 · Opening Weekend
  We’re going to open on Saturday at eight. We’ve told the whole street already.
    ~ Vamos a abrir el sábado a las ocho. Ya se lo hemos dicho a toda la calle.
  Are you going to make bread that morning?
    ~ ¿Vas a hacer pan esa mañana?
  I’m going to bake at five with my grandfather.
    ~ Voy a hornear a las cinco con mi abuelo.
  I’m going to bring the old photos for the wall. There are pictures of the bakery from 1974.
    ~ Yo voy a traer las fotos antiguas para la pared. Hay fotos de la panadería de 1974.
  I’m going to write the menu on the blackboard.
    ~ Yo voy a escribir el menú en la pizarra.
  Who is going to serve the coffee?
    ~ ¿Quién va a servir el café?
  You are. You’re going to learn today, in two hours.
    ~ Tú. Vas a aprender hoy, en dos horas.
  Two hours? That’s not going to be enough. I’m going to break at least two cups.
    ~ ¿Dos horas? Eso no va a ser suficiente. Voy a romper por lo menos dos tazas.

## ep08 · A Promise to the Library
  The library will lend you twenty books. They are old, but nobody reads them here. Will you keep them safe?
    ~ La biblioteca te prestará veinte libros. Son antiguos, pero aquí no los lee nadie. ¿Los cuidarás?
  I will put a small list of books on every table, so people can read the titles before they sit down.
    ~ Pondré una lista pequeña de libros en cada mesa, para que la gente lea los títulos antes de sentarse.
  People will read them here, not at home?
    ~ ¿La gente los leerá aquí, no en casa?
  They’ll read here. If somebody takes one, I’ll replace it.
    ~ Leerán aquí. Si alguien se lleva uno, lo repongo.
  I think this will work. Nobody in this street will steal a book.
    ~ Creo que esto funcionará. Nadie en esta calle robará un libro.
  And I’ll bring the list to the library every month. I’ll tell you which books people choose.
    ~ Y traeré la lista a la biblioteca cada mes. Te diré qué libros elige la gente.
  Then we’ll start on Monday.
    ~ Entonces empezamos el lunes.
  Thank you. This will change the room.
    ~ Gracias. Esto va a cambiar la sala.

## ep09 · What We Have Already Done
  We’ve painted the walls and we’ve cleaned every tile. We started at six and it’s almost midnight.
    ~ Hemos pintado las paredes y hemos limpiado cada baldosa. Empezamos a las seis y es casi medianoche.
  I’ve moved the table three times.
    ~ Yo he movido la mesa tres veces.
  I’ve written the menu twice, because Leo has changed the prices.
    ~ Yo he escrito el menú dos veces, porque Leo ha cambiado los precios.
  Grandpa has already put his key on the wall.
    ~ El abuelo ya ha puesto su llave en la pared.
  I’ve waited twenty years for that small nail. Your grandmother kept the key in a drawer.
    ~ He esperado veinte años por ese clavito. Tu abuela guardaba la llave en un cajón.
  We haven’t tested the coffee machine yet.
    ~ Todavía no hemos probado la máquina de café.
  We have tested it. You just haven’t tasted the coffee. The first cup is yours tomorrow.
    ~ Sí la hemos probado. Tú es que no has catado el café. La primera taza es tuya mañana.
  Then I haven’t finished my work today.
    ~ Entonces no he terminado mi trabajo de hoy.

## ep10 · The Menu Test
  We have some bread, some soup and a banana cake. My grandfather made the cake this morning.
    ~ Tenemos pan, sopa y un pastel de banano. Mi abuelo hizo el pastel esta mañana.
  Is there any coffee left?
    ~ ¿Queda café?
  There isn’t any coffee. There’s some tea and a little milk.
    ~ No queda café. Hay té y un poco de leche.
  Are there any plates in the box?
    ~ ¿Hay platos en la caja?
  There are some plates, but there aren’t any clean cups. Somebody has to wash them before eight.
    ~ Hay platos, pero no hay tazas limpias. Alguien tiene que lavarlas antes de las ocho.
  My brother wants some cake. He asked twice. He’s waiting outside with his bike.
    ~ Mi hermano quiere pastel. Lo ha pedido dos veces. Está esperando fuera con la bici.
  He can have some cake if he washes some cups.
    ~ Puede tomar pastel si lava unas tazas.
  He says that’s not a fair offer, but he’s already washing.
    ~ Dice que no es una oferta justa, pero ya está lavando.

## ep11 · The Budget Jar
  We have a lot of ideas and very little money. The jar is the only accounting we have.
    ~ Tenemos muchas ideas y muy poco dinero. El bote es toda nuestra contabilidad.
  How much is in the jar?
    ~ ¿Cuánto hay en el bote?
  Ninety dollars. That’s not much for a whole month, and the rent takes half of it.
    ~ Noventa dólares. No es mucho para todo un mes, y el alquiler se lleva la mitad.
  We have a few cups, but we don’t have many plates.
    ~ Tenemos unas cuantas tazas, pero no tenemos muchos platos.
  You need fewer plates and more patience. A café is not a shop full of plates.
    ~ Necesitáis menos platos y más paciencia. Un café no es una tienda llena de platos.
  We spend too much on milk. I’ll buy less.
    ~ Gastamos demasiado en leche. Compraré menos.
  And a little more bread. People always want bread, and it’s the cheapest thing on the menu.
    ~ Y un poco más de pan. La gente siempre quiere pan, y es lo más barato del menú.
  Bread is the only thing we make ourselves.
    ~ El pan es lo único que hacemos nosotros.

## ep12 · Rules for Opening Day
  We must wash our hands before we make any food. That rule is not negotiable.
    ~ Tenemos que lavarnos las manos antes de preparar comida. Esa norma no se discute.
  Do I have to wear the apron all day?
    ~ ¿Tengo que llevar el delantal todo el día?
  You have to wear it in the kitchen. You should wear it outside too. The apron is also the uniform.
    ~ Tienes que llevarlo en la cocina. Fuera también deberías. El delantal es también el uniforme.
  Must I write the prices in dollars?
    ~ ¿Tengo que escribir los precios en dólares?
  You have to. And you mustn’t change them on Saturday. We agreed on those prices last night.
    ~ Sí. Y no puedes cambiarlos el sábado. Acordamos esos precios anoche.
  You should open the door five minutes early.
    ~ Deberíais abrir la puerta cinco minutos antes.
  Why five minutes?
    ~ ¿Por qué cinco minutos?
  Because somebody is always waiting outside. Somebody always was. I opened five minutes early for thirty years.
    ~ Porque siempre hay alguien esperando fuera. Siempre lo hubo. Yo abrí cinco minutos antes durante treinta años.

## ep13 · If It Rains Again
  If it rains on Saturday, what will we do? The forecast says rain all weekend.
    ~ Si llueve el sábado, ¿qué hacemos? El pronóstico da lluvia todo el fin de semana.
  If it rains, we’ll move the small tables inside. The big table stays where it is.
    ~ Si llueve, metemos las mesas pequeñas dentro. La mesa grande se queda donde está.
  And if the room is full?
    ~ ¿Y si la sala se llena?
  If the room is full, people will wait at the library. Mrs. Ruiz has already offered the reading room.
    ~ Si la sala se llena, la gente esperará en la biblioteca. Mrs. Ruiz ya ha ofrecido la sala de lectura.
  If nobody comes, I’ll eat all the cake myself.
    ~ Si no viene nadie, me como yo todo el pastel.
  If nobody comes, we’ll still open on Sunday.
    ~ Si no viene nadie, abrimos igual el domingo.
  They’ll come. If they smell the bread, they’ll come. That worked in 1974 and it works now.
    ~ Vendrán. Si huelen el pan, vendrán. Eso funcionó en 1974 y funciona ahora.
  Then we’ll open the window early.
    ~ Entonces abrimos la ventana temprano.

## ep14 · Why I Started
  Why did you decide to open this place?
    ~ ¿Por qué decidiste abrir este sitio?
  I enjoy meeting people. I love listening to their stories. Every customer arrives with one.
    ~ Disfruto conociendo gente. Me encanta escuchar sus historias. Cada cliente llega con una.
  You could work in any café for that.
    ~ Para eso podrías trabajar en cualquier café.
  I wanted to keep my grandfather’s corner. I hate seeing it empty. It was closed for two years and nobody cared.
    ~ Quería conservar la esquina de mi abuelo. Odio verla vacía. Estuvo cerrada dos años y a nadie le importó.
  Do you regret leaving your old job?
    ~ ¿Te arrepientes de haber dejado tu antiguo trabajo?
  I miss having a fixed salary. I don’t miss the noise. Some months I earn less than before.
    ~ Echo de menos tener un sueldo fijo. No echo de menos el ruido. Algunos meses gano menos que antes.
  And you’ve learned to bake.
    ~ Y has aprendido a hornear.
  I’ve learned to wait. Bread taught me that. You can’t hurry bread.
    ~ He aprendido a esperar. Eso me lo enseñó el pan. Al pan no se le mete prisa.

## ep15 · The First Real Morning
  The first customers came in quietly and looked around slowly. Nobody said a word for five minutes.
    ~ Los primeros clientes entraron en silencio y miraron despacio. Nadie dijo una palabra en cinco minutos.
  Two students walked straight to the book list and read it carefully. They asked if they could take one to the table.
    ~ Dos estudiantes fueron directos a la lista de libros y la leyeron con cuidado. Preguntaron si podían llevar uno a la mesa.
  You served them quickly. You didn’t drop anything.
    ~ Los serviste rápido. No se te cayó nada.
  I moved carefully. That’s not the same as fast.
    ~ Me moví con cuidado. Eso no es lo mismo que rápido.
  The room worked well. People spoke softly, like in a library. Even the coffee machine sounded quieter.
    ~ La sala funcionó bien. La gente hablaba bajito, como en una biblioteca. Hasta la máquina de café sonaba más suave.
  The bread sold easily. It always did.
    ~ El pan se vendió fácil. Siempre fue así.
  And Max behaved perfectly all morning.
    ~ Y Max se portó perfectamente toda la mañana.
  Max slept perfectly. That’s different.
    ~ Max durmió perfectamente. Eso es otra cosa.

## ep16 · The First Review
  Have you ever read a review about yourself? This one has fourteen lines and your name in the first one.
    ~ ¿Has leído alguna vez una reseña sobre ti? Esta tiene catorce líneas y tu nombre en la primera.
  I’ve never seen my name on a website before.
    ~ Nunca había visto mi nombre en una página web.
  It’s on the neighbourhood page. Somebody wrote it last night.
    ~ Está en la página del barrio. Alguien la escribió anoche.
  Has anyone said anything bad?
    ~ ¿Alguien ha dicho algo malo?
  Nobody has. One person has asked for more chairs. They came on Saturday, they say.
    ~ Nadie. Una persona ha pedido más sillas. Vinieron el sábado, dicen.
  We’ve never had more than twelve chairs. Twelve is what fits between the tables.
    ~ Nunca hemos tenido más de doce sillas. Doce es lo que cabe entre las mesas.
  I have never trusted reviews. I trust the empty plates.
    ~ Yo nunca me he fiado de las reseñas. Me fío de los platos vacíos.
  Then look at the kitchen. There are no plates left. Not one came back with food.
    ~ Pues mira la cocina. No queda un solo plato. Ni uno volvió con comida.

## ep17 · The Missing Key
  I was serving table four when the key disappeared. It hangs there every single day.
    ~ Estaba atendiendo la mesa cuatro cuando desapareció la llave. Está colgada ahí todos los días.
  Where was it while you were working?
    ~ ¿Dónde estaba mientras trabajabas?
  It was hanging on the wall. Then it wasn’t there.
    ~ Estaba colgada en la pared. Y luego ya no estaba.
  I was cleaning the shelf when I saw an empty nail. I thought somebody had moved it to the office.
    ~ Yo limpiaba el estante cuando vi el clavo vacío. Pensé que alguien la había llevado a la oficina.
  So somebody took it while we were busy.
    ~ O sea que alguien la cogió mientras estábamos liados.
  A customer came back at four. She was smiling and she had the key in her hand.
    ~ Una clienta volvió a las cuatro. Venía sonriendo y traía la llave en la mano.
  The key fell into her bag when she was paying.
    ~ La llave se le cayó en el bolso mientras pagaba.
  She walked back twenty minutes to return it. She said it belonged on that wall.
    ~ Volvió andando veinte minutos para devolverla. Dijo que su sitio era esa pared.

## ep18 · The Community Map
  This map is the most colourful thing on the wall. It has more colours than the menu.
    ~ Este mapa es lo más colorido de la pared. Tiene más colores que el menú.
  Every customer adds one place. It grows faster than we expected. We started it with three streets and a pencil.
    ~ Cada cliente añade un sitio. Crece más rápido de lo que esperábamos. Lo empezamos con tres calles y un lápiz.
  The taxi driver added the best spot for the sunset.
    ~ El taxista añadió el mejor sitio para ver el atardecer.
  The library is smaller on the map than in real life. Nobody has offered to correct it.
    ~ La biblioteca es más pequeña en el mapa que en la realidad. Nadie se ha ofrecido a corregirlo.
  Somebody drew the park bigger than the whole street.
    ~ Alguien dibujó el parque más grande que toda la calle.
  That’s the most honest map in this city.
    ~ Ese es el mapa más honesto de esta ciudad.
  It’s more useful than the app on my phone.
    ~ Es más útil que la aplicación de mi teléfono.
  And much more beautiful. I hope nobody ever fixes the scale.
    ~ Y mucho más bonito. Espero que nadie arregle nunca la escala.

## ep19 · A New Saturday Plan
  Next Saturday we’re going to invite three artists. Two of them teach at the school and one paints walls.
    ~ El sábado que viene vamos a invitar a tres artistas. Dos dan clase en el colegio y uno pinta paredes.
  If the weather is good, they’ll paint outside. The wall by the window is the best canvas.
    ~ Si hace buen tiempo, pintarán fuera. La pared de la ventana es el mejor lienzo.
  And if it rains, we’ll use the back room.
    ~ Y si llueve, usamos la sala de atrás.
  I’m going to make a playlist for the afternoon. Nothing louder than a conversation.
    ~ Voy a preparar una lista de canciones para la tarde. Nada más alto que una conversación.
  The library will lend you twenty more books for the event.
    ~ La biblioteca os prestará veinte libros más para el evento.
  If this works, we’re going to do it every month.
    ~ Si esto funciona, lo hacemos todos los meses.
  Monthly? That’s going to be a lot of bread. Sixty loaves, maybe more.
    ~ ¿Mensual? Eso va a ser mucho pan. Sesenta hogazas, quizá más.
  Then we’ll start baking on Friday night.
    ~ Pues empezamos a hornear el viernes por la noche.

## ep20 · The Door Stays Open
  You’ve worked for six months without one free Saturday. Your grandmother would have said the same thing.
    ~ Has trabajado seis meses sin un solo sábado libre. Tu abuela habría dicho lo mismo.
  I’ve learned more here than in three years of classes. And I have never been so tired.
    ~ He aprendido más aquí que en tres años de clases. Y nunca he estado tan cansada.
  You still have to learn to manage the money.
    ~ Todavía tienes que aprender a manejar el dinero.
  I know. I’ve decided to take a business course in January. Ana found it and signed me up already.
    ~ Lo sé. He decidido hacer un curso de gestión en enero. Ana lo encontró y ya me apuntó.
  The library has a free one on Tuesday evenings.
    ~ La biblioteca tiene uno gratis los martes por la tarde.
  And we’ve found four volunteers for the weekends.
    ~ Y hemos encontrado cuatro voluntarios para los fines de semana.
  Then the door can stay open. That’s all I wanted.
    ~ Entonces la puerta puede seguir abierta. Era lo único que quería.
  To Sam’s Corner! And to the next six months!
    ~ ¡Por Sam’s Corner! ¡Y por los próximos seis meses!

## en-a2-community-garden-email

  We will meet at the main gate at nine thirty and finish around one.
  First, we are going to remove dry leaves and prepare four planting areas.
  After that, volunteers can choose between painting the wooden signs and planting herbs.
  Please wear old clothes and closed shoes.
  Bring gardening gloves if you have them, but we can lend tools and gloves to twenty people.
  The weather forecast says there may be light rain, so we will work unless there is a storm.
  Children are welcome with an adult.
  At midday, the café across the street will provide soup and bread.
  To help us plan the food, reply to this email before Thursday and tell us how many people are coming.
  Thank you, Lina, Garden coordinator.

## en-a2-cooking-class-reminder
  The community centre cooking class starts next Tuesday at six thirty.
  This month we are learning to make vegetable dumplings and a simple dipping sauce.
  The centre will provide bowls, knives, and all ingredients, but students should bring a clean apron and a container for leftovers.
  Please tell the tutor about allergies when you arrive.
  The kitchen has only eight workstations, so students who cannot attend should cancel by Monday morning.
  Beginners are welcome, and the tutor demonstrates each step slowly before anyone tries it.
  Because places are limited, the centre keeps a short waiting list and opens a second group on Thursday whenever enough people ask.
  The class ends at eight, and everyone can stay for a short tasting session.

## en-a2-first-job-interview
  Before her first job interview, Noor wrote down examples of times when she had solved a problem, helped a customer, or learned a new task.
  She did not memorise long answers because she wanted to sound natural.
  The evening before the interview, she checked the bus route and prepared two questions about the role.
  At the office, the manager asked about teamwork and reliability.
  Noor gave one clear example for each question and admitted when she did not know a technical word.
  When she was unsure about one technical term, she explained how she would find the answer instead of guessing, and the manager seemed to appreciate that honesty.
  She left without knowing the result, but she felt prepared because she had shown how she worked rather than only describing her qualities.

## en-a2-museum-audio-guide
  The City Museum has introduced a free audio guide for visitors aged twelve and over.
  Visitors can borrow a small player at the entrance or download the guide before arriving.
  The guide has twelve stops, but visitors do not need to follow them in order.
  Each stop lasts between three and five minutes and includes one question to discuss with a companion.
  The museum says the guide is designed to help visitors notice details, not to replace the labels beside the objects.
  Staff can also lend headphones to visitors who prefer to listen alone, and a printed version is available for anyone who finds small screens difficult.
  The museum plans to add a second language to the guide later this year.
  Families with younger children can use the picture trail instead.

## en-a2-new-neighbour-welcome

  The residents’ group would like to welcome them without making the first week too busy.
  On Saturday 3 June, we will leave a small basket at each door with local information, tea, and a map.
  In the afternoon, volunteers can show the families the recycling room, the bus stop, and the shared garden.
  Please do not visit their flats unless they invite you.
  If you can help, reply with one hour when you are free.
  The group also asks everyone to keep the two visitor parking spaces free during that weekend, so the removal vans have room to unload.
  Anyone with spare boxes or garden pots can leave them, clearly labelled, in the hall.
  We will make a rota and send it on Monday.

  From two until six, it will stop at Oak Street and the sports centre, but it will not stop at Hill Farm or the old bridge.

## en-a2-recycling-collection-plan
  The residents of Brookfield used to put recycling bags outside on different days, and the collection team often missed some streets.
  Last month, the council tested a clearer plan.
  Each street received a colour and a collection day.
  Blue streets put paper and cardboard outside on Monday, while green streets put glass and metal outside on Tuesday.
  A reminder card explained what could not go in the bags.
  After four weeks, fewer bags contained the wrong materials, but some residents said the colour system was confusing at night.
  During the trial, whenever a bag was rejected, the team left a short note explaining the reason instead of simply leaving it on the pavement.
  The council will add brighter signs before deciding whether to keep the plan.

## en-a2-school-exchange-diary
  When I arrived in Leeds for the school exchange, I expected to feel lonely.
  Instead, my host family invited me to a neighbourhood football match on the first evening.
  At school, my partner, Josh, showed me the library and explained the timetable.
  I understood most lessons, although I needed extra time with the science vocabulary.
  On Wednesday, our class visited a local theatre.
  I missed my parents that night, but I wrote them a message and felt better.
  To remember new words, I started a small notebook and reviewed five of them each night before sleeping.
  Josh laughed at my pronunciation at first, but he soon began correcting me gently instead.
  By Friday, I could order lunch, ask for directions, and join conversations without translating every sentence in my head.

## en-a2-weekend-volunteer-shift
  I signed up for a Saturday shift at the food bank because I wanted to understand how local support works.
  At nine, the coordinator showed us the storage room and explained the labels.
  My first job was checking dates on tins.
  Later, I helped pack boxes for families with different needs.
  One box needed baby food, while another needed ingredients for a vegetarian meal.
  I had expected the work to be quiet, but the room became busy when a delivery arrived.
  Before leaving, I wrote down three ways to volunteer again.
  The coordinator explained that the busiest hours were usually just after lunch, when new donations and waiting families often arrived at the same time.
  The shift was tiring, yet it made the community’s organisation visible to me.

## en-a2-weekend-without-my-phone
  Last Saturday, Maya left her phone at home by accident.
  She noticed it when the bus arrived, but she did not go back.
  At first, she felt nervous because she could not check the map or message her friend Sam.
  She asked the driver where to get off and wrote the stop name on a paper ticket.
  At the market, Maya waited beside the flower shop, their usual meeting place.
  Sam arrived ten minutes later.
  Without their phones, they looked carefully at the stalls, talked to the sellers, and tried a new fruit drink.
  Maya bought a small notebook to record places she wanted to visit.
  On the bus home, she realised that the day had felt longer and calmer.
  She still uses her phone every day, but now she sometimes leaves it in her bag when she meets friends.
